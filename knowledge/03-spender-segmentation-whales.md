# Spender Segmentation

## The segments

Free-to-play revenue is extremely concentrated. The standard segmentation:
**whales** (top spenders, ~1% of installs), **dolphins** (moderate spenders,
~4%), **minnows** (small/one-off spenders, ~5%), and **free users** (~90%,
never pay). Overall payer conversion for hybrid-casual sits around
**8-15%** of installs, with ~9-10% a common midpoint.

## Revenue concentration

The famous heuristic: **~1% of players (whales) contribute roughly half of
IAP revenue**. This asymmetry drives most monetization design: whale
retention and whale-targeted offers matter disproportionately, and losing a
single whale hurts more than losing hundreds of free users.

## Typical LTV ranges

In this platform's calibration (matched to its real purchase backbone):
minnows realize roughly **$1-10** lifetime spend, dolphins **$10-30**, and
whales **$25-60+**. Boundaries used for labeling are LTV > $10 → dolphin and
LTV > $20 → whale. Ranges deliberately overlap — a $28 spender could have
been driven by either behavioural profile.

## Strategy per segment

Whales: high-tier offer packs ($20-50 price points), VIP treatment, never
interrupt with forced ads. Dolphins: mid packs, conversion nudges toward
higher tiers. Minnows: entry-price offers ($1-5), ad-supported play is
acceptable. Free users: rewarded-video monetization only — pushing IAP at
users with near-zero payment propensity damages retention for no revenue.
